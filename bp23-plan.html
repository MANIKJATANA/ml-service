The problem we're fixing

The product quietly gathers the data to answer its own hardest questions — and reads almost none of it.
Three that matter most: (1) the "delivery rate" measures the button you clicked (did we announce?), not the audience (did anyone actually open their photos?) — a school can read 100% "announced" while almost nobody looked. (2) Every time a teacher confirms or rejects a match, we save that verdict — the ground truth for "is matching getting better or worse?" — and we never total it up, so a rising wrong-match rate is invisible. (3) Pricing rides on how many teachers a school has, yet we don't show when each teacher last signed in, we hide who created an event, and we don't record who uploaded a photo at all — and that last one can't be recovered later, so every day we wait loses more of it.
Your call (already made): take all four groups below, in one phase. It's a bigger, server-side build than the recent app-only ones; the two rounds of self-review are the safety net.

What we're building — four groups, one phase

AAttribution — stop losing the history the urgent one1 DB change
  • Record who uploaded each photo — a new "uploaded by" stamp on every photo from now on. This is the one database change; the value of it grows every day it's on, and it can't be backfilled.
  • Show when each teacher last signed in — a "Last sign-in" column on the staff list (we already record it; we just never showed it). Now you can see who's actually using their seat.
  • Show who created an event — a "Created by" line on the event page (again, already stored, just hidden).
BMeasure the flagship claims 2 of the 3 Highs
  • Honest reach — beside "Announce rate", a new "Open rate": of the events you announced, how many had at least one student open their photos. So "we announced everything" can no longer hide "nobody looked." (An exact per-event "what % of each audience opened" needs a bigger change and is noted as out-of-scope — see the honest limits.)
  • "Saved a photo" rate — how many students have actually saved at least one of their own photos (a real "did it land?" signal).
  • An engagement line that can go down — a month-by-month trend of first-time opens, so a drop-off is visible instead of a single all-time number that only ever grows.
  • Is matching accurate? — a new "Quality" section: the confirm rate (of the matches staff reviewed, how many they kept) and the separate wrong-person rate, month by month — so accuracy trends become visible. This shows the numbers; it doesn't change the matching model (that's a separate, parked project).
CAnswers behind the numbers
  • "Which students?" lists — filter the students list to "never signed in" or "never opened their photos", and make the dashboard alerts click through to the exact filtered list (instead of a dead-end number).
  • A per-child engagement card — on a student's page: events they appear in, photos opened, last opened, downloads. So a parent's "did my child get their pictures?" is answerable at a glance.
  • A richer notify roster — beside "Opened", show downloads and a persistent "first opened" date (the current "Opened" resets when you re-announce; this one doesn't).
DEstate view — which schools are stuck platform admin only
  • An "age" axis on the adoption funnel — school age, days to first delivery (how long from signup to actually sending photos), "not started", and "no event since …" — plus a sortable table — so you can spot a school that signed up and stalled, and step in.
A note on doing this right: a design pass caught two traps and steered around them — the honest "reach" number is computed without ever cross-wiring the matching engine to the rest of the database (a hard architectural line we keep), and the per-child engagement lives on its own quick screen so it never slows the everyday student pages. Both are folded into the plan.

How we'll work

One phase — all four groups, then the full check (server: format + types + tests + the layering rule; app: types + lint + build), the one database change tested on a throwaway database (never your live one), and a 2× self-review (round 1 correctness + tenant safety + the no-cross-wiring rule; round 2 "are the numbers honest?"), then brought back to you. Nothing is committed until you say so.

How we'll check it

The full gate stays green, the database change applies and rolls back cleanly on a throwaway copy, and every new number has a real-database test proving its math + that it only ever counts one school's data. Then a walk: a newly-uploaded photo records its uploader; the staff list shows Last sign-in; the event page shows Created-by; the analytics show Open-rate + Saved + the first-opens trend + the Quality section; a dashboard alert clicks through to the right filtered list; a student's page shows the engagement card; the roster shows downloads + first-opened; and the estate funnel shows the age columns and sorts.

Not in this build (noted honestly): the exact audience-weighted open rate (what % of each event's audience opened) needs a bigger recording change — we ship "events with ≥1 opener" now; storage/cost tracking and true history-over-time lines stay parked; "no event since …" is our honest stand-in for "stalled" (there's no dedicated activity clock yet); and improving the matching model itself stays parked (BP15) — BP23 shows the accuracy numbers, it doesn't change the matching. One database change (a nullable "uploaded by" column), no ML change, no new permission, no new dependency.
